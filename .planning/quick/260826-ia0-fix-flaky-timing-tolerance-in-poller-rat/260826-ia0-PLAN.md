<objective>
Eliminate a CI-only timing flake in the two poller rate-limit tests by subtracting a small, named, documented jitter tolerance from their elapsed-span floor — and then prove empirically that the reduced floor is still a real gate.

Purpose: GitHub Actions run 32997261111 (job `test`, commit 082a19a on main) failed with:

```
--- FAIL: TestMusicBrainzCycle_ConcurrentPollingStaysInsideRateLimit (0.14s)
    poller_test.go:2098: elapsed span between first and last call = 139.873132ms, want >= 140ms
```

Both tests drive 8 calls through a `rate.Limiter` at `rate.Limit(50)` / burst 1 (20ms per token) and assert the first-to-last timestamp span is at least `7 * 20 * time.Millisecond` — exactly 140ms, with zero margin. The observed 139.873132ms is ~0.13ms (0.09%) under an *exact* theoretical multiple. That is scheduler/timer-wakeup granularity on a loaded shared CI runner, not a rate-limiter regression: the timestamps are recorded *after* `limiter.Wait` returns, so the measured span absorbs goroutine wake-up latency on both ends, and `rate.Limiter` makes no guarantee that the observed post-Wait span reproduces its internal delay computation to the nanosecond. Local full-suite runs pass consistently.

Output: one changed test file — one new package-level constant and two changed `wantMin` expressions. Plus a throwaway sentinel probe (Task 2) that is verified and then reverted, never committed.

Scope discipline — do NOT touch:
- `internal/detection/musicbrainz.go` or anything else related to the deluxe recheck-window work from quick task 260826-gj8. Unrelated; this flake is purely a timing-tolerance issue.
- The `maxInFlight == 5` assertion or the `len(timestamps) == 8` assertion in either test. Those are the assertions that keep the elapsed-span check non-vacuous (a purely sequential loop would also clear the time floor); weakening them would be the *actual* dangerous change here.
- Production code in `internal/poller/poller.go`, the `rate.Limiter` construction in `internal/httpclient`, or any real rate-limit configuration. The limiter is behaving correctly — the test's measurement tolerance is what is wrong.
- The `rateLimitedReleaseGroupSource` / `rateLimitedAlbumSource` fixtures' `inFlight`/`maxInFlight` CAS bookkeeping or their timestamp recording. Their post-`Wait` recording order is deliberate and documented.
- Any attempt to "fix" this by retrying, `t.Skip`-ing on CI, or removing the span assertion entirely. The assertion earns its keep — it is the only thing empirically proving the shared limiter serialises concurrent workers (PERF-02, 11-RESEARCH.md Pitfall 5).
</objective>

In the PERF-02 section of internal/poller/poller_test.go — directly beneath the `// --- PERF-02: rate ceiling and overlap guard under concurrency ---` section header and above the `rateLimitedReleaseGroupSource` type declaration — add a single package-level constant named `rateLimitSpanJitterTolerance` with the value `2 * time.Millisecond`.

Give it a doc comment stating all four of these facts, so a future reader never mistakes it for an arbitrary fudge factor:
1. What it is subtracted from: the theoretical first-to-last span floor asserted by the two `ConcurrentPollingStaysInsideRateLimit` tests.
