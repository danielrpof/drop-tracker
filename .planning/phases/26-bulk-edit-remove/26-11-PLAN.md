<objective>
Let the user remove one tag from every selected artist in one action, with an Undo that restores it to exactly the
artists that had it. The tracer opens the bar's remove-tag picker, picks a tag carried by part of the selection, and
watches one request remove it. Task 2 adds Undo and the 404 path. Task 3 pins the picker's options, counts, empty states,
and keyboard contract.

Purpose: BULK-02's remove half on the client, and BULK-05 for it. This plan completes the bar's five controls.
Output: `bulkRemoveTag`, `removableTags`, the bar's Remove tag and inline picker, the route handler and Undo, and tests.

Locked inputs: D-12 (remove-tag offers only tags carried by the selection), D-13, D-15 (Undo is for remove-tag only), D-22
(tag id; 404 → failure toast + refresh), U1, and U4 (Undo through bulk add-tag by name to `applied`). 26-UI-SPEC.md "Inline
tag pickers (D-12)" (Remove tag), "Bulk remove tag" (including Undo), and the Copywriting "Inline tag pickers" and "Toasts"
remove/restore rows are verbatim contracts.

Wire contract (plan 26-07): `POST /watchlist/bulk/tags/remove` with `{"ids":[...],"tag_id":N}` answers 200
`{"applied":[...],"carried":N,"skipped":[{"id","reason":"gone"}]}`, or 404 `{"error":"tag not found","code":"tag_not_found"}`.
`applied` is exactly the entries whose link was removed.

are 1–3 lines with at most one decision reference. Tag names render as plain JSX text only.

| Threat ID | Category | Component | Severity | Disposition | Mitigation Plan |

| T-26-42 | Denial of Service (usability) | A stale tag id after a concurrent delete or merge | low | mitigate | 404 → failure toast plus `refresh()`, with the selection kept (D-22) |
