<objective>
Close G-25-12. Apply the user's 2026-10-12 decision on review WR-03 ("anchor"): when the toggled chip's card leaves the list, focus
moves to a chip label on the fallback anchor card instead of falling to <body>.

Gap (25-UAT.md G-25-12, verifier probe in 25-VERIFICATION.md): the UI-SPEC focus table defines only "Toggled, card still visible".
When the clicked card unmounts, its focused label button goes with it and `document.activeElement` becomes BODY. A keyboard user is
then sent back to the top of the tab order. The 25-08 truth ("keeps focus on the label button") can't hold once the label unmounts.

Locked decision (binding). Cite it as "user decision 2026-10-12 (G-25-12)": focus moves to the fallback anchor card's chip label,
and the fallback card is the one plan 25-19 resolves (depends on G-25-11).

Scope (binding, user decision 2026-10-12 on the plan checker's follow-up): keyboard only. Focus relocation fires only for a
keyboard-initiated toggle, Enter or Space on the focused label. A mouse click that removes a card leaves focus alone. A held-focus
check alone can't tell them apart, because Chromium and Firefox focus a button on mouse click.

Claude's discretion (recorded):
- Detect keyboard activation with the click event's `detail === 0`, read in TagChips' label `onClick` and passed through
  `toggleFilterTag` to the capture. `detail` is the click count, so a mouse or touch click reports 1 or more, and Enter or Space
  activation of a button reports 0. It is stateless and per event. Tracking the last pointerdown versus keydown instead would need
  document listeners and goes stale for a click that fires neither. A programmatic `click()` also reports 0, so the held-focus and
  focus-fell-to-the-document checks stay as guards.
- Prefer a pressed chip label, as the gap asks. After a chip-click filter every surviving card carries a still-selected tag, because
  the click clears stickiness (G4), so a pressed label exists in practice. The any-label step is a guard.
- When no card survives, focus the `Your artists` heading with tabIndex -1. The heading stays mounted in both the list and the
  filtered-to-zero states, and the review named it as the alternative. The empty state's Clear filters button would put focus on an
  action the user didn't choose.
- The hook reports the lost focus through an optional callback wrapped in React 19.2's `useEffectEvent`. The effect still depends on
  `key` alone, and the route keeps the knowledge of chip labels and the heading.

Purpose: keyboard users keep their place in the list after a toggle that removes the card they were on.
Output: the keyboard flag from the chip label to the capture, the hook's focus-lost callback, the chip-label marker attribute, the
route's focus target with the heading fallback, tests, and the new UI-SPEC focus table row.
</objective>
