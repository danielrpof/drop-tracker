Per D-14 (the newest `new_release` date per artist; its retention clause is superseded by G1), G2, and G3, extend both
ListWatchlist and GetWatchlistEntry in `queries/watchlist.sql` identically:
- After the `tag_names` column, select `latest.release_date AS latest_release_date` and `upcoming.release_date AS next_release_date`.
- Between `JOIN artists a ON a.id = w.artist_id` and the trailing `ORDER BY a.name ASC, a.id ASC` (ListWatchlist) or
  `WHERE w.id = $1` (GetWatchlistEntry), add two lateral joins over `events e`, both filtered on `e.artist_id = a.id`,
  `e.event_type = 'new_release'`, and `e.release_date IS NOT NULL`:
  - `latest`: adds `e.release_date <= left(to_char((now() AT TIME ZONE 'UTC')::date, 'YYYY-MM-DD'), length(e.release_date))`,
    then `ORDER BY e.release_date DESC LIMIT 1`, joined `ON true`.
  - `upcoming`: the same with `>` and `ORDER BY e.release_date ASC LIMIT 1`, joined `ON true`.
  Truncating UTC today to the stored value's own length is what makes precision fall out: `2026` vs `2026` is not upcoming,
  `2026-11` vs `2026-10` is.
- Select the plain `release_date` column from each lateral. An aggregate over the lateral makes sqlc emit `interface{}` or a
  non-null `string`, and every artist without releases then fails the NULL scan with a 500 (RESEARCH Pitfall 1).
- No retention argument. `watchlist.NewService` keeps its current signature (G1).
- One comment above the joins in each query, at most three lines, citing G1–G3. Leave the existing header comments as they are.
